# Exceptional authenticator recovery and operational purge

Technical proposals for review, September 18, 2026. No recovery grant, factor removal, session revocation, purge worker, retention policy or hosted operation is implemented or enabled by this document. Existing ordinary MFA, membership suspension/reinstatement and the 30-day recoverable organization-deletion window remain as accepted. Authority and identity proof are settled in `APPROVED_FOLLOW_ON_DECISIONS.md` and `B09_B10_NEXT_TECHNICAL_PROPOSAL.md`.

## Exceptional authenticator loss

Preserve the approved reviewers: Organization Admins or Super Admins for Organizers/Editors/Viewers; only an Organization Super Admin for organization Admins/Super Admins. Organization rank takes precedence over production rank. Require recent MFA from the assisting actor, no self-approval, and verification through a previously known phone number or in person. Email alone cannot establish identity. Do not introduce a second ordinary reviewer. Escalate all-Super-Admins-locked-out cases to the Platform Super Admin and notify organization Super Admins. Routine factor replacement and Platform Super Admin self-recovery are outside this proposal.

Recommended execution contract:

1. Create a restricted recovery case with immutable case ID, organization, target account, approving actor, authority revision, proof method/time/outcome and short reason. Store no factor secrets, codes, raw identity documents or unnecessary phone details. A server-only preflight checks other active memberships and platform designation. Return only “platform review required” to the organization reviewer; never reveal other organizations or platform details.
2. For an ordinary single-organization target, an authorized approval creates a one-use, 15-minute authorization bound to case, actor, organization, target and proof revision. Fifteen minutes is a proposed operational expiry, not an existing product decision. Store a digest of the random capability. Never put it in ordinary access tokens, links logged by the application, or a general tenant session. An expired approval requires fresh authorization; request retries cannot extend expiry.
3. A restricted server worker claims the authorization atomically and rechecks live authority, target scope, state and expiry. It first places an account recovery hold recognized by every protected application admission path. Approval alone grants no application access. The hold is necessary because the authenticator belongs to the account; its exact account-wide impact requires review before implementation, including for multi-organization targets.
4. Execute a journaled provider sequence: revoke affected sessions, retire old verified factors, establish replacement-only enrollment, verify the new factor, revoke any temporary recovery access, and require a fresh normal login. The account hold remains until all required steps have positive completion evidence. No step may fall back to ordinary AAL1 tenant access. **Provider support and ordering are unproven here.** A separately approved implementation spike must demonstrate these capabilities using fictional accounts without the prohibited B08 token-redemption race probe. If the provider cannot safely restrict enrollment or establish revocation, do not ship this mechanism; return a concrete supported alternative for review.
5. Use an operation ID and monotonically advancing step journal. Exact retries inspect the recorded/provider state and resume only the missing step; a changed target or payload conflicts. Lost responses must not issue a second unrestricted credential or duplicate factor. Failures retain the hold and a support-visible next action. Success cannot be reported merely because factor deletion returned 200.
6. Audit non-secret approval and step outcomes; queue affected-user notification through the existing administration outbox. Notification failure remains visible/retryable and does not undo a completed security operation. Restricted platform review owns cross-organization/platform-designated cases; organization approval alone never authorizes their account-wide reset.

Acceptance must demonstrate denied authority/self-approval/expired case, known-phone or in-person proof record, no tenant access with replacement-only credentials, old-factor/session denial, fresh-login success, retry after each interrupted step, target/actor mismatch denial, retained drafts after normal reauthentication, and mail-sink failure/retry. Existing B08 exception remains unchanged; `scripts/test-account-token-race.ts` and equivalent provider/token race probes remain prohibited.

**Narrow review needed:** approve or revise the account hold plus restricted enrollment mechanism and its expiry; define platform authority/impact for multi-organization or platform-designated targets. Reviewer roles and identity-proof choices are not reopened. Actual provider capability verification and implementation need their own bounded dispatch.

## Permanent organization purge

The local read-only catalog in `evidence/b15-review/purge-dependency-catalog.json` inventories **47 public/private tables and 115 foreign keys** at the B13/B14 schema. It contains schema metadata, not document content. These 47 include shared account/platform tables that must not be treated as tenant-owned. Auth/provider schemas and external stores require separate disposition. A foreign-key cascade alone is insufficient: JSON receipt payloads, imported record mappings, shared template provenance and external copies can retain tenant data.

| Disposition group | Current stores / required handling |
|---|---|
| Document content | `schedules`, `schedule_versions`, `schedule_snapshots`, snapshot imports/copies/receipts/policies, template rows/sources/bindings/browser origins and template receipts. Include trashed/archived objects, history and document copies inside JSON. |
| Sharing and transfers | Client links/aliases, copy receipts, snapshot-copy receipts, transfer requests/writes. Invalidate recipient resolution first. Detect cross-organization references and legitimate independent destination copies; do not silently delete another tenant's schedule. Review provenance handling before releasing a held purge. |
| Organization hierarchy/access | Invitations, schedule restrictions, production/organization memberships, production days, phases, productions, presentation/MFA settings and their receipts, organization lifecycle and organization row. Delete dependents in catalog-derived reverse dependency order, then verify no dangling references. |
| Administrative/migration material | Audit events, administration outbox, membership/lifecycle receipts, migration runs/records/versions, workflow requests. Classify every JSON field and retained export/artifact. Remove tenant payload; retain only an explicitly approved minimal completion record. |
| Shared account/platform state | Profiles, Auth users/factors/sessions, account policy acceptances, activity/mail limits, platform operators, global policy versions and global rate controls. Never blindly delete these with a tenant. Preserve unrelated memberships and identity; any orphan-account cleanup is a separate reviewed operation. |
| External copies | Actual Storage/blobs, provider backups, local/cloud migration exports, mail delivery, logs and browser downloads. Inventory ownership and retention before activation. A downloaded file outside service control cannot be promised erased by server purge. No external asset inventory was performed in B15. |

Recommended operation protocol:

1. An operations owner submits a dry run only after database time reaches the existing deadline. Acquire the same organization policy lock as cancellation; verify `awaiting_purge`, lifecycle version, no applicable retention hold, and the separately approved operation authorization. The 30-day window is not extended or shortened by retries.
2. Produce a manifest of tenant keys, counts, byte counts where available, dependency edges, JSON-bearing records, external object keys, shared-reference exceptions, holds and backup expiry obligations. Hash the manifest. Unknown table/column or dependency drift fails closed and requires review; do not infer scope from table names alone.
3. Review that exact manifest and its exceptions. Recommended operator is a designated platform operations role with recent MFA, distinct from ordinary tenant scheduling roles; this operational authority is proposed, not approved. Bind execution to manifest hash, organization/lifecycle revision and stable purge operation ID. Recheck under the lock immediately before execution. Cancelled, stale, held or not-yet-expired organizations cannot run.
4. First record a durable deletion tombstone outside the tenant database/backup being purged, then disable all tenant resolution. Execute bounded idempotent steps in dependency order with step receipts and retryable checkpoints. External deletion confirmations need their own receipts; a partial failure remains visibly incomplete and frozen. Never claim atomicity across Postgres and external services.
5. Reconcile manifest counts and residual queries after each stage, including JSON references and public alias/link lookups. Record skipped shared identities and held exceptions explicitly. Completion means the agreed live-service scope is erased and residual exceptions/backup expiry dates are disclosed, not that every backup has vanished.
6. Any restore must reconcile the external tombstone ledger and reapply completed purges **before** enabling application/Auth access to recovered tenant content. Rehearse a restore containing a purged organization and prove it remains inaccessible. The B14 same-schema restore rehearsal does not yet prove this future tombstone step.

Proposed minimum tombstone: organization identifier (or stable keyed identifier), operation ID, completion time, manifest digest, disposition-policy version and residual expiry obligations; no document bodies, member directory or raw notification content. Store it with independent access control and backup. Retention must outlive every restorable backup and operational replay source; do not silently select a numeric retention period while actual hosted backup horizons and holds are unknown.

**Narrow release decisions:** name the operational owner/authorized executor; approve the shared-reference and audit/receipt exceptions; set retention and hold disposition against actual provider backup/export horizons; approve tombstone ownership and restore enforcement. These remain concrete release gates, not assumed indefinite deferrals. No worker should be enabled until each value is recorded and a fictional interrupted/resumed purge plus tombstone-aware restore passes. Do not reopen the settled product cancellation window or use these pending decisions to block independent local parity work.
